AI RACING GAME - VIDEO SCRIPT (ENGLISH)

INTRODUCTION
------------
Duration: 30 seconds

Voiceover:
"This project is an AI-powered kart racing game built in Unity. It combines two machine learning
systems: reinforcement learning bots that learn to drive the circuit, and a Random Forest model
that gives each bot its own power-up strategy. At the end of each race, a third classifier tells
the player what kind of driver they are."

Show:
- A short race start sequence with countdown.
- The track, the player kart, and the AI bots.
- The results screen at the end showing the player behavior label.


GAMEPLAY OVERVIEW
-----------------
Duration: 1 minute 30 seconds

Voiceover:
"The player races against AI bots on a kart circuit. The bots navigate the track autonomously.
They pick up and use power-ups during the race. The game records what the player does each lap.
At the finish line, the player learns what type of racer they are."

Main points:
- The player and bots race together on the same circuit.
- Bots drive using a trained reinforcement learning model.
- Bots use power-ups using a separate machine learning model.
- The game silently collects the player's lap data throughout the race.
- The results screen shows the final standings and the player's behavior profile.

Show:
- A full race: player and bots driving, using power-ups, overtaking.
- The HUD: lap count, race position, and current power-up.
- The results screen appearing after the race with the player classification line.


REINFORCEMENT LEARNING — BOT DRIVING
--------------------------------------
Duration: 2 minutes

Voiceover:
"The bots learn to drive using reinforcement learning. The agent is trained with PPO —
Proximal Policy Optimization — inside Unity ML-Agents. During training, the agent observes
its speed, the direction to the next checkpoint, nearby walls through raycasts, and its
progress around the track. It earns rewards for reaching checkpoints in the correct order
and is penalized for collisions, going the wrong way, or sitting still."

Main points:
- Algorithm: PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) via Unity ML-Agents.
- Observations: speed, direction to next checkpoint, distance, grounded state, ray sensors.
- Positive rewards: passing checkpoints in order, moving toward the next checkpoint.
- Penalties: hitting walls, idling, going in reverse, leaving the track.
- Training converged around 180,000 steps; the final reward stabilized at 64.0.
- The trained model is exported as an ONNX file and loaded back into Unity at runtime.

Show:
- An early training run: the bot spinning, crashing, going backwards.
- A late training run: the bot completing clean laps.
- A TensorBoard reward curve showing the learning progression.
- Caption: "RL agent learns the circuit from scratch."


POWER-UP DECISION MODEL — BOT ITEM STRATEGY
--------------------------------------------
Duration: 2 minutes

Voiceover:
"Bots also need to decide which power-up to use and when. This is handled by a second,
completely separate machine learning model: a Random Forest classifier trained in Python
and exported as a JSON file that Unity reads at runtime."

Main points:
- Every 0.25 seconds, each bot observes five things: whether it is on a straight section,
  how many enemies are ahead, how many are behind, banana skins in its path, and shells
  approaching from behind.
- The Random Forest takes those five values and predicts the best power-up:
  shell to attack, banana to defend, mushroom to boost on a straight, or star against
  multiple threats.
- There are three trained bot profiles. The balanced bot uses all power-ups equally.
  The aggressive bot favors shell and banana attacks. The pacifist bot prefers mushroom
  boosts and star defense.
- A fourth model, imitar_player, is trained directly on the human player's own power-up
  history. A bot using this model will tend to make the same item choices the player makes.
- The model runs entirely in C# with no Python dependency at runtime.

Show:
- A bot picking up a power-up and immediately using it based on context.
- A side-by-side showing aggressive vs pacifist bot behavior with power-ups.
- Brief caption: "Random Forest decides which item the bot uses, every 0.25 seconds."


DATA COLLECTION AND PLAYER BEHAVIOR CLASSIFICATION
---------------------------------------------------
Duration: 2 minutes

Voiceover:
"Throughout the race the game records nine metrics about the player for every lap: lap time,
finishing position, wall collisions, and the count of each power-up used or received.
When the race ends, those numbers are aggregated across all laps and fed into a third
machine learning model: the player behavior classifier."

Main points:
- The recorder component attaches automatically to the player kart at runtime.
- At the end of the race it computes three extra features: aggressiveness, hit ratio,
  and power-up efficiency — twelve features in total.
- A Random Forest classifier, trained on synthetic race data, maps those twelve features
  to one of six player archetypes: Pro, Aggressive, Conservative, Chaotic, Clumsy, or Slow.
- The result appears on the results screen as a single line: for example,
  "Player profile: Aggressive (84%)".
- This classification is purely informational. It does not change how the bots drive
  or which power-ups they use in that race.

Show:
- The results screen after the race, highlighting the player profile line.
- A brief table showing the six archetypes and what each one means.
- Caption: "After the race, the game tells you what kind of driver you are."

Voiceover:
"The six archetypes cover the main ways people play: the fast and consistent Pro, the risky
Aggressive driver, the careful Conservative, the unpredictable Chaotic, the collision-prone
Clumsy, and the simply Slow. The classifier was trained on over two thousand synthetic race
sessions and validated with real player data."
